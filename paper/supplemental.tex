\section{Short-pulse response simulation}

The deterministic response calculation uses a 10-ns, sine-squared $\pi/2$
pulse with a 0.5-ns time step. Figure~\ref{fig:s2-response} separates the
response-calibration and simulated held-out grids and compares the dissipative
master-equation response with local response models. The shaded interval is a
simulation-defined candidate interval, not a hardware validation range.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
 \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{fig2/figS2-response-simulation.pdf}
 \caption{Deterministic short-pulse response evidence. The fitted and ideal
 full-kernel cubic routes provide separate coefficient cross-checks; the model
 fold bounds the continuous local inverse.}

\section{Analytic-truth frequency-recovery audit}

Figure~\ref{fig:s3-frequency} maps the physically realizable candidate
detunings to the local flux branch of the analytic transmon dispersion. The
dispersion is withheld from all estimators and used only to define the
simulation truth and plotting coordinate. The wider rotating-frame detuning
grid in Fig.~\ref{fig:s2-response} is not relabeled as flux where that branch
has no corresponding physical point.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
 \includegraphics[width=0.72\textwidth]{fig2/figS3-frequency-recovery-simulation.pdf}
 \caption{Analytic-truth audit of local frequency recovery. The upper axis
 compares the cubic-kernel estimate with withheld analytic truth; the lower
 axis reports signed residuals for three estimators. The calculation is
 deterministic with $\sigma=0$ by construction.}
 \label{fig:s3-frequency}
\end{figure}

\section{Experimental extensions}

An experimental implementation should add binomial shot sampling, readout
assignment correction, repeated calibrations under controlled drift, and a
range-guard transition to Ramsey reacquisition.  Coefficients $A_1$ and $A_3$
should be remeasured after material pulse-shape or drive-chain changes.
