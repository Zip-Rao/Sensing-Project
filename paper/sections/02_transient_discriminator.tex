\section{Short-pulse frequency discriminator}
\label{sec:discriminator}

\subsection{Driven transmon}

We consider a flux-tunable transmon at bias $\Phi$ with transition frequency
$\omega_q(\Phi)$.  In a frame rotating at the drive frequency $\omega_d$ and
under the rotating-wave approximation, the two-level Hamiltonian is
\begin{align}
 H(t)&=\frac{\Delta}{2}\sigma_z+\frac{\Omega(t)}{2}
 \left[\cos\varphi(t)\sigma_x+\sin\varphi(t)\sigma_y\right],\nonumber\\
 \Delta&=\omega_q(\Phi)-\omega_d.
 \label{eq:hamiltonian}
\end{align}
The flux bias is the controlled variable and $\Delta$ is the quantity measured
within each feedback iteration.

The discriminator uses two zero-delay Ramsey sequences,
\begin{align}
 \mathcal S_+ &: R_y(\pi/2)R_x(\pi/2),\\
 \mathcal S_- &: R_y(\pi/2)R_{-x}(\pi/2).
 \label{eq:sequences}
\end{align}
Each branch contains the same state-preparation pulse and an analysis pulse
whose phase differs by $\pi$.  If $p_+$ and $p_-$ are their final excited-state
probabilities, we define
\begin{equation}
 p_{\mathrm d}=\frac{p_+-p_-}{2}.
 \label{eq:pdiff}
\end{equation}
The branch symmetry cancels the even powers of detuning near resonance, giving
\begin{equation}
 p_{\mathrm d}=A_1\Delta+\frac{A_3}{3!}\Delta^3+O(\Delta^5).
 \label{eq:cubic-response}
\end{equation}
Unlike a conventional Ramsey scan, this signal is generated entirely during
the control pulses.  One frequency estimate therefore requires two driven
evolutions after the response coefficients have been calibrated.

\subsection{Local inverse and fold}

The linear estimator is $\widehat\Delta_1=p_{\mathrm d}/A_1$.  The cubic
estimator solves Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic-response} for the root continuously
connected to $\Delta=0$.  When $A_1A_3<0$, the cubic response has a turnover at
\begin{equation}
 |\Delta_{\mathrm{fold}}|=\sqrt{-\frac{2A_1}{A_3}}.
 \label{eq:fold}
\end{equation}
Beyond this point, the same population difference can correspond to multiple
detunings and no local polynomial correction can make the measurement globally
invertible.  The implementation therefore rejects the distant cubic root and
falls back to the linear estimate outside the calibrated branch.  This guard
prevents a numerical root finder from returning a plausible but physically
disconnected solution; it does not extend the capture range.
